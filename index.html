Job Tracker

Built from scratch Next.js · TypeScript · SQLite · AI

A desktop app I built to run my own job search. It tracks every application on a board, and it goes out and finds new openings on its own, filtered down to roles I am actually eligible for. It currently manages a live pipeline of 369 roles across 5 countries.

  • It searches for me. Checks company career sites first, then runs broad discovery for employers I have not seen. Every result is re-validated in code, not trusted: postings older than 30 days, senior titles, roles that refuse visa sponsorship and anything I previously rejected all get dropped automatically.
  • It measures the search like a process. Daily goal tracking, streaks, a 30-day activity chart, interview and offer conversion rates, and average response time. The same instinct as putting KPIs on a production line.
  • It runs on zero budget. No paid API. It drives the Claude CLI already installed on my machine, so there is no per-token bill. When one run started consuming 60% of my available usage, I traced the cost to session count rather than prompt size, merged six search sessions into one and cut it by roughly 70%.
  • It respects privacy. Everything is local. No account, no server, no analytics. My resume and application history never leave my disk.

Line Simulator

Little's Law · drag the sliders

165
Units in system (WIP)

Little's Law: WIP = Throughput × Cycle Time. Push the rate up or let the cycle time slip, and inventory on the floor climbs with it. That is cash sitting on a conveyor, longer lead times, and more places for a defect to hide. Most of the wins on my resume came from attacking one side of this equation.

The finish decides the route, and hard matte runs the first two tanks in reverse: nitric for oil removal before the long caustic etch, then nitric again. Caustic dwell is what separates glossy from matte. Short dwell leaves a surface you can polish to a shine, long dwell etches it, and once etched you cannot polish it back.

Anodizing is the constraint and its time scales with the spec, so the customer's micron number sets what the line can do that day. Our rate rose with the spec too, but nobody ever checked whether it rose far enough. A kilo at 20 microns eats 2.36 times the tank time of a kilo at 12, and that ratio is the one a rate card should be tested against.
